Quick summary

The definitive playbook for modern restaurant operators: shift your focus from email campaigns to in-moment capture. Standardize the ask, intercept complaints in real-time, and hold your floor staff accountable.

Rule 1: Move the ask from the inbox to the table

The old playbook relied on email receipts and SMS surveys sent the day after a meal. The new playbook requires asking for the review in the restaurant, right when the guest feels the most satisfied.

Friction destroys conversion. By using NFC technology, you reduce a multi-step digital search down to a single physical tap.

The Golden Window

The highest intent to review occurs in the 5 minutes after the bill is settled, before the guest has left the building.

Rule 2: Catch the bullet before it hits Google

Not every guest leaves happy, but almost every guest is willing to complain privately if you give them a fast, easy way to do so.

A core part of your playbook must be a 'defensive layer.' You need a system that detects a 1- or 2-star rating before it posts, routes it to a private form, and alerts a manager immediately.

Rule 3: Individual accountability drives volume

If 'everyone' is responsible for asking for reviews, no one actually does it. Your playbook needs clear attribution.

Assign specific NFC cards to specific servers. This allows managers to see precisely who is generating reviews and who needs coaching on their close-out script.

  • Do not rely on a communal QR code at the host stand
  • Track the number of 'taps' generated per shift by server
  • Coach servers who have high taps but low review conversions (indicates a poor ask or rushing)

Rule 4: Stop responding defensively to public reviews

When a bad review does slip through, the playbook demands a calm, standardized response. Apologize for the specific miss, invite them back offline, and never argue in public.

Remember, your response isn't really for the angry reviewer—it's for the next 1,000 potential guests who read that review to see how management handles pressure.
